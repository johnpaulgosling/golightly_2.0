\section{Simulation studies}
\label{sec:sim}

Two checks are reported. The first is the linear Gaussian identity, on the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck pilot already stored in the results files. The second is the Lotka--Volterra comparison of Section~5.2 of \citet{golightly2026}, at the reduced settings fixed before the run. All algorithms were coded in base R and the Lotka--Volterra runs used R~4.3.3 on an Intel Xeon processor. The pilot is not a reproduction of the 2026 tables.

\subsection{Identity and the surrogate diagnostic}

The identity check uses the scalar Ornstein--Uhlenbeck diffusion of Section~5.1 of \citet{golightly2026}, with \(M = 60\) parameter particles, 16 observations, a fixed ensemble of size 12 and four replicate series. The gold-standard chain on each series has 3000 iterations after a burn-in of 1000. Those choices are smaller than Section~5.1. When the observation variance inside the filter equals the observation variance of the data, \(R_g = R = 0.1\), the log ensemble Kalman increment and the log Rao-Blackwellised increment agree. The maximum absolute difference on each replicate is 0. Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} is the corresponding statement. The correction does not change the weight in this case, and the extra density evaluations have nothing to correct.

The same pilot records the surrogate diagnostic. The mean, over four replicates, of the median absolute difference between a nearest-neighbour increment and a fresh ensemble Kalman increment is 0.277. The threshold fixed before the run was 1. The diagnostic stays inside that threshold. It is not a uniform bound on the log-likelihood, and it is not substituted into the total-variation calculation of Section~\ref{sec:methods}.

The pilot also compared an exact tree with a list and a time-based kernel switch with two fixed kernels, and it compared a finite-difference proposal with a random walk. Those runs are not part of the claim.

\subsection{Lotka--Volterra targets}
\label{sec:lv}

The latent process is the prey--predator diffusion (25) of \citet{golightly2026}, with drift
\[
a(x, \theta)
  = \bigl(\theta_1 x_1 - \theta_2 x_1 x_2,\ 
          \theta_2 x_1 x_2 - \theta_3 x_2\bigr)
\]
and with the diffusion matrix printed in that section. The observation model is (27): the prey component is observed with a Gaussian density whose mean and variance both equal that component, and the predator component is latent. The truth is \(x_0 = (50, 50)^{\top}\) and \(\theta = (0.5, 0.0025, 0.3)^{\top}\). The prior is independent, \(\theta_1 \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(2, 4)\), \(\theta_2 \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(20, 10^{4})\) and \(\theta_3 \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(2, 4)\), in the shape-rate parameterisation. The source paper writes \(\mathrm{Gamma}(a, b)\) and does not name the parameterisation.

The synthetic path uses Euler--Maruyama with time step \(10^{-3}\) on \([0, 40]\) and is thinned by 2000. Twenty prey observations are kept, at times \(2, 4, \ldots, 40\). The initial condition is treated as known. Inside each filter the transition between observations is ten Euler steps of length 0.2. A coordinate below \(10^{-6}\) is replaced by \(10^{-6}\). The source paper does not state a boundary rule.

Three filters are run on this one series, with four algorithm replicates. The plain nested filter uses \(\tilde{R} = H\hat{\mu}_{t\mid t-1}\) and targets \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\). The Rao-Blackwellised filter multiplies that variance by 2, evaluates \eqref{eq:rb}, and targets \(\tilde{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\). Bootstrap SMC2 uses the Euler--Maruyama transition as its proposal and the observation density (27) as its state weight. For that discretisation its parameter target is \(\pi_t\). Each filter uses \(M = 60\) and \(\gamma = 0.4\). The ensemble size is fixed at \(N = 12\) for the first two filters. SMC2 uses 24 state particles. There is no delayed acceptance and no growth of \(N\). The rejuvenation proposal is one random walk, with \(\zeta^{2} = 2.56^{2}/3\). Section~5.2 of \citet{golightly2026} used \(M = 1000\), an ensemble that starts at 20 and then grows, 50 replicates, delayed acceptance with three neighbours, and a particle Markov chain Monte Carlo gold standard of \(10^{6}\) iterations. None of those settings is used here. SMC2 is the reference for \(\pi_t\), and it is a noisy one at this state-particle count.

The reported summary is the terminal weighted mean of \(\log\theta_j\). Table~\ref{tab:lv} gives the average of that summary over the four replicates, with the replicate standard deviation in parentheses.

The pre-registered comparison is the plain-filter mean minus the SMC2 mean, judged against the standard error of that difference. For \(\log\theta_2\) the difference is \(-0.0586\), with standard error 0.0865. For \(\log\theta_3\) the difference is \(-0.0354\), with standard error 0.0953. Both differences are negative, so the point estimates lie in the direction reported by \citet{golightly2026}, and neither absolute difference exceeds its standard error. The shift is unresolved. It is not reported as appearing, and it is not reported as failing to appear. The Rao-Blackwellised mean is closer to the SMC2 mean on \(\log\theta_2\), where the absolute gap is 0.0055 against 0.0586, and not on \(\log\theta_3\), where the absolute gap is 0.0446 against 0.0354. The reduction on both coordinates, which the rule asked for, does not hold.

For \(\log\theta_1\), which had no pass mark, the plain-filter mean exceeds the SMC2 mean by 0.1227, with standard error 0.0688. The Rao-Blackwellised gap on that coordinate is 0.0152. The mean number of proposals is 405 in each filter, and the mean numbers of acceptances are 79.25, 76.25 and 83.25. The largest fraction of Euler draws that met the floor is 0.008064, below the limit 0.01 fixed in the design. The draw counts in Table~\ref{tab:lv} do not include the observation-density evaluations inside \eqref{eq:rb}, so they understate the cost of the Rao-Blackwellised weight relative to the plain filter.
