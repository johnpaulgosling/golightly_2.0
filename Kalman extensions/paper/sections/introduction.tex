\section{Introduction}

State-space models are used when a latent Markov process is observed with noise. The parameter \(\theta\) is static. The likelihood of a series is an integral over the latent path, and that integral is rarely available in closed form. Sequential Monte Carlo squared (SMC2) explores the sequence of parameter posteriors by weighting parameter particles with an unbiased particle-filter estimate of each likelihood increment, and by moving the particles with a particle Metropolis--Hastings kernel when the effective sample size falls \citep{chopin2012}. The variance of that estimate grows with the length of the series and, more sharply, with the observation dimension.

The ensemble Kalman filter replaces the forecast distribution by a Gaussian distribution whose moments are the sample moments of an ensemble, and it updates the ensemble with a Kalman shift. The corresponding likelihood contribution is the Gaussian marginal of that forecast. It is not unbiased for the true likelihood. \citet{golightly2026} place this contribution inside the SMC2 recursion and move the parameter particles with one step of ensemble Markov chain Monte Carlo. The resulting nested ensemble Kalman filter targets the parameter posterior induced by the ensemble Kalman likelihood, which we denote \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\), rather than the exact posterior.

The resample-move step is the expensive part of the recursion. At time \(t\) it reruns the ensemble filter from the start of the series for every proposal. \citet{golightly2026} screen proposals with a \(k\) nearest-neighbour surrogate of the ensemble Kalman log-likelihood, and they note several further possibilities: a faster neighbour search, a choice among kernels, a gradient proposal, and the use of the surrogate in the weight as well as in the screen. They also give a Rao-Blackwellised weight for a nonlinear observation model. The discussion illustrates a regime of small parameter dimension and larger observation dimension. Those remarks are the starting point of this paper. We do not treat them as theorems.

Our contribution is to state the target of each modification and to check the claims that can be checked on a linear Gaussian pilot. Firstly, the delayed-acceptance kernel is unchanged when a tree and a list return the same neighbours. Secondly, a choice among kernels that is made before the proposal, and that uses only the time index, preserves \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\) when each kernel does. Thirdly, a nearest-neighbour incremental weight targets the posterior under that surrogate, not \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\). Fourthly, when the true observation density equals the linear Gaussian density used by the filter, the Rao-Blackwellised weight equals the ensemble Kalman weight. A finite-difference approximation of the gradient of the ensemble Kalman log-likelihood can sit inside a corrected proposal; on the pilot it is not competitive per transition draw, and we do not recommend it.

The numerical section uses a scalar Ornstein--Uhlenbeck diffusion with a known Kalman likelihood. The pilot uses 60 parameter particles, 16 observations and an ensemble of size 12. It is not the experiment reported by \citet{golightly2026}.
