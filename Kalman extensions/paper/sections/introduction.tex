\section{Introduction}

State-space models are used when a latent Markov process is observed with noise and the parameter \(\theta\) is static. The likelihood of a series is an integral over the latent path, and that integral is rarely available in closed form. Sequential Monte Carlo squared (SMC2) propagates a cloud of parameter particles, reweights them by an unbiased particle-filter estimate of each likelihood increment, and moves them with a particle Metropolis--Hastings kernel when the effective sample size falls \citep{chopin2012}. The variance of that estimate grows with the length of the series and, more sharply, with the observation dimension.

The ensemble Kalman filter replaces the forecast distribution by a Gaussian distribution whose mean and covariance are the sample moments of an ensemble, and it updates the ensemble with a Kalman shift. The likelihood contribution is the Gaussian marginal of that forecast. It is not unbiased for the true likelihood. \citet{golightly2026} place this contribution inside the SMC2 recursion and move the parameter particles with one step of ensemble Markov chain Monte Carlo. The nested ensemble Kalman filter therefore targets the parameter posterior induced by the ensemble Kalman likelihood, denoted \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\), rather than the exact posterior.

The resample-move step dominates the cost. At time \(t\) the ensemble filter is rerun from the start of the series for every proposal. \citet{golightly2026} screen proposals with a \(k\) nearest-neighbour surrogate of the ensemble Kalman log-likelihood. The same discussion points to a faster neighbour search, a choice among kernels, a gradient proposal and the use of the surrogate in the weight as well as in the screen. It also gives a Rao-Blackwellised weight for a nonlinear observation model. It also illustrates a regime of small parameter dimension and larger observation dimension. Those remarks are the starting point for the calculations below.

Our contribution is to name the target of each modification and to compare the modifications on a linear Gaussian pilot with a known likelihood. (i)~The delayed-acceptance kernel is unchanged when a tree and a list return the same neighbours. (ii)~A kernel chosen before the proposal, as a function of the time index alone, preserves \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\) when each candidate kernel does. (iii)~A nearest-neighbour incremental weight targets the posterior under that surrogate, which we denote \(\pi_{\mathrm{knn}}\). (iv)~When the true observation density equals the linear Gaussian density used by the filter, the Rao-Blackwellised weight equals the ensemble Kalman weight. A finite-difference approximation to the gradient of the ensemble Kalman log-likelihood can be used inside a Metropolis--Hastings proposal. On the pilot its effective sample size per transition draw is worse than that of the random walk, and we do not recommend it.

The pilot is a scalar Ornstein--Uhlenbeck diffusion. It uses 60 parameter particles, 16 observations and an ensemble of size 12, with four replicate series. These settings are smaller than the experiment in Section~5.1 of \citet{golightly2026}, and the gold-standard chains are shorter than the chains reported there.

The remainder of this paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} describes the neighbouring algorithms and what each implies for the nested filter. Section~\ref{sec:methods} sets out the targets of the modifications. Section~\ref{sec:sim} reports the pilot, and Section~\ref{sec:disc} discusses what the pilot supports and what remains open.
