\section{Introduction}

State-space models are a standard description of a latent Markov process observed with noise. A static parameter governs the transition, the observation, or both. Applications include predator--prey dynamics, epidemic incidence and systems with many observed components \citep{golightly2026,temfack2026}. The inferential target at time \(t\) is the posterior distribution of the static parameter given the observations so far. The observed-data likelihood that enters that posterior is an integral over the latent path, and the integral is rarely available in closed form.

Sequential Monte Carlo squared (SMC2) propagates a cloud of parameter particles. Each particle carries a particle filter, the particle filter supplies an estimate of the likelihood increment, and a particle Metropolis--Hastings kernel moves the parameter particles when the effective sample size falls \citep{chopin2012}. The estimate is unbiased for the true increment, and the parameter marginal of the extended target is the exact posterior for any fixed number of state particles. The variance of the estimate grows with the length of the series and, more sharply, with the observation dimension. In that regime a particle filter can require a number of state particles that is prohibitive.

The ensemble Kalman filter replaces the forecast distribution by a Gaussian distribution whose mean and covariance are the sample moments of an ensemble, and it updates the ensemble with a Kalman shift. The likelihood contribution is the Gaussian marginal of that forecast. The contribution is exact for the Gaussian approximation and is biased for the true predictive density of the state-space model. \citet{drovandi2022} place the contribution inside a Metropolis--Hastings kernel on a fixed data set. \citet{golightly2026} place it inside the SMC2 recursion and call the resulting scheme the nested ensemble Kalman filter. The parameter target is the posterior induced by the ensemble Kalman likelihood, which we denote \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\), rather than the exact posterior \(\pi_t\). For a nonlinear or non-Gaussian observation the same construction first replaces the observation density by a linear Gaussian approximation. A Rao-Blackwellised weight then puts the true observation density back into the increment, whilst the forecast remains Gaussian, and targets a third posterior, \(\tilde{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\).

On the Lotka--Volterra diffusion in Section~5.2 of \citet{golightly2026}, prey are observed under a Gaussian density whose variance equals the latent prey, and predators are latent. That paper reports that the plain filter shifts the posterior means of the predation coefficient and the predator death rate downward, because the observation variance is replaced by a function of the forecast mean, and that the Rao-Blackwellised filter reduces the discrepancy at greater cost. The comparison uses 1000 parameter particles, an ensemble whose size is allowed to grow, delayed acceptance, 50 replicates and a long particle Markov chain Monte Carlo run as the reference. The same paper uses a nearest-neighbour average as a screen inside delayed acceptance, so that many proposals avoid an ensemble Kalman likelihood. The discussion lists a further use of that average: as the incremental weight itself, at a fixed ensemble size, which would give a cheaper filter at the expense of accuracy. The posterior induced by that weight is not defined there, and no distance between it and \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\) is given.

Those omissions matter, because the two replacements are different errors. The first is the ensemble Kalman likelihood against the true likelihood, and hence \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\) against \(\pi_t\). The second is a nearest-neighbour prediction against the ensemble Kalman likelihood. A weight that uses the prediction targets a further posterior, which we denote \(\pi_{\mathrm{knn}}\). A screen that is corrected by the ensemble Kalman likelihood leaves \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\) invariant. A comparison that scores \(\pi_{\mathrm{knn}}\) against \(\pi_t\), or against a gold-standard run for \(\pi_t\), attributes both errors to the ensemble Kalman approximation. The Rao-Blackwellised weight sits in between, in a precise sense. It removes the linear Gaussian approximation of the observation and retains the Gaussian forecast, so \(\tilde{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\) is not \(\pi_t\). When the true observation density is already the linear Gaussian density inside the filter, the ratio that is meant to correct the observation equals one, and the Rao-Blackwellised weight reproduces the ensemble Kalman weight. \citet{golightly2026} compare the two weights in examples where the observation is not linear and Gaussian. The identity says that the comparison is informative in that case, and that the correction has no inferential content when the observation model inside the filter is already the right one.

Our contribution is to separate the errors and to say which posterior each modification targets.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] We name the four targets and keep them distinct. Bootstrap SMC2 targets \(\pi_t\). The plain nested filter targets \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\). The Rao-Blackwellised weight targets \(\tilde{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\). The nearest-neighbour weight targets \(\pi_{\mathrm{knn}}\). Figure~\ref{fig:targets} is the corresponding diagram.
\item[(ii)] We record the delayed-acceptance argument that leaves \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\) invariant when the neighbour average is only a screen, and the elementary total-variation bound that would control \(\pi_{\mathrm{knn}}\) if the two log-likelihoods differed by a uniform constant. The hypothesis of that bound is not met. The ensemble Kalman log-likelihood is redrawn with the ensemble, and the diagnostic we compute is not such a constant.
\item[(iii)] We prove that the Rao-Blackwellised increment equals the ensemble Kalman increment when the two observation densities agree. The identity is specific to that case. A linear Gaussian illustration with a deliberately wrong observation variance shows that the correction can move the posterior means when the densities differ.
\item[(iv)] On the Lotka--Volterra observation we compare the plain filter, the Rao-Blackwellised filter and bootstrap SMC2. The design is smaller than Section~5.2 of \citet{golightly2026}, and the reference is SMC2 itself. The downward shift in the two log rate parameters lies inside its standard error.
\end{enumerate}
The numerical sections state every setting that differs from \citet{golightly2026}, including the shape-rate reading of the gamma priors and the treatment of non-positive Euler coordinates. They are not a reproduction of the tables in that paper. We do not analyse an epidemic series. \citet{temfack2026} already study a nested ensemble scheme for incidence, and they are explicit that a Gaussian assumption remains.
