\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

We have separated two errors that a nested ensemble Kalman filter can make, and we have kept the posteriors they induce distinct from the exact posterior and from each other. \citet{golightly2026} already define the plain filter and the Rao-Blackwellised filter, compare them with SMC2 on a Lotka--Volterra diffusion, and use a nearest-neighbour average as a screen inside delayed acceptance. Their discussion suggests the further step of using that average as the weight. The present paper makes that separation. A screen corrected by the ensemble Kalman likelihood remains a sampler for \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\). A weight that is the surrogate is a sampler for \(\pi_{\mathrm{knn}}\). Scoring the second against SMC2, or against a gold-standard run for \(\pi_t\), would attribute the surrogate error to the ensemble Kalman approximation. The unbiased correction of a plug-in Gaussian density, as in \citet{drovandi2022} and \citet{temfack2026}, removes a different error again and does not restore \(\pi_t\).

Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} is the linear Gaussian case of the Rao-Blackwellised weight. On the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck series the two log increments agreed exactly when the filter used the observation variance of the data, so the correction is not worth its cost in that case. The same series with the filter variance set to 0.5, against data generated at variance 0.1, gives the complementary illustration: the Rao-Blackwellised mean absolute error was 0.169 and the plain-filter error was 0.369. The correction can move the posterior means when \(f\) and \(g\) differ, and the identity says that this movement is not available when they agree. \citet{golightly2026} compare the two filters on observations that are not linear and Gaussian. The identity supplies the case in which that comparison would be empty, which their applications do not isolate.

The surrogate diagnostic stayed inside the threshold fixed in advance. The mean of the median absolute log differences was 0.277, against a threshold of 1. On the same series the run that replaces non-reference weights by the surrogate had mean absolute error 0.293, against 0.172 for the plain filter and 0.126 for delayed acceptance, and it used 5217 transition draws against 33855 and 23970. The accuracy-cost comparison in Figure~\ref{fig:ou} is the content of the second error at this particle count: fewer forecasts, and a larger error in the posterior means, because the target has changed. Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} states the total-variation bound that would hold for two fixed log-likelihoods differing by at most \(\delta\). The ensemble Kalman log-likelihood is not a fixed function, the diagnostic is not a uniform \(\delta\), and no Lipschitz constant on the gamma prior was available. We therefore do not claim a distance between \(\pi_{\mathrm{knn}}\) and \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\).

On the Lotka--Volterra series the plain filter's means of \(\log\theta_2\) and \(\log\theta_3\) lie below the SMC2 means, by 0.0586 and 0.0354. The standard errors are 0.0865 and 0.0953. Under the comparison fixed before the runs, the shift is unresolved. Figure~\ref{fig:lv} shows the same fact: the standard-error bars on those two coordinates include zero. The Rao-Blackwellised filter is closer to SMC2 on \(\log\theta_2\) and further from it on \(\log\theta_3\). \citet{golightly2026} report a downward shift for the plain filter, reduced by the Rao-Blackwellised weight, at \(M = 1000\) and against a long particle Markov chain Monte Carlo run. Our comparison uses \(M = 60\), four replicates and SMC2 itself as the reference. It can show a shift of the size in their Table~2, and it can miss it. Here it does not resolve it. The result does not confirm that ordering, and it does not contradict it. What it adds is a statement about the scale of the discrepancy relative to Monte Carlo error when the ensemble is small and the reference is SMC2, which their table does not report.

The limits of the Lotka--Volterra comparison are those of the study itself. There is one series. The state-particle count in SMC2 is fixed at 24, and a particle Markov chain Monte Carlo reference was not run. The standard errors are estimated from four replicates. A small fraction of Euler draws met the positive floor. The Euler counts omit the cost of the density ratio in the Rao-Blackwellised weight. The gamma priors use a shape-rate reading that \citet{golightly2026} do not state. These are reasons to treat the unresolved verdict as a statement about this comparison. The linear Gaussian gold-standard chains are likewise short, so the mean absolute errors in Section~\ref{sec:ou} are relative to those chains.

We anticipate that the separation is of most use where a practitioner is choosing among the plain filter, the Rao-Blackwellised filter and a surrogate weight, and would otherwise read every discrepancy from \(\pi_t\) as a defect of the ensemble Kalman likelihood. The correction is the wrong expense when the observation model is already linear and Gaussian. It is a relevant expense when a state-dependent variance is replaced by a function of the forecast mean, which is the Lotka--Volterra observation, whether or not a particular finite-\(M\) comparison resolves the shift. The surrogate weight is a different estimator, of a different posterior, and the screen is not a substitute for it.

A bound on \(\pi_{\mathrm{knn}}\) that can be checked, for a likelihood that is redrawn with the ensemble, remains open. So does a repeat of the Lotka--Volterra comparison at the particle counts of Section~5.2 of \citet{golightly2026}. Gradient proposals inside the rejuvenation step, and a neighbour search whose cost scales more gently with the number of stored particles, are among the modifications suggested by \citet{golightly2026}. They change the move under a target that has already been chosen. They do not decide which of \(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\), \(\tilde{\pi}_{\mathrm{enkf}}\) and \(\pi_{\mathrm{knn}}\) that target should be. We do not add an epidemic series. \citet{temfack2026} already study a nested ensemble scheme for incidence.
