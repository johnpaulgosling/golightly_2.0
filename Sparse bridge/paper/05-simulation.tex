\section{Simulation}
\label{sec:simulation}

The study uses the synthetic susceptible-infected-removed mechanism of Section 4.1 of \citet{whitaker2025}. The initial state is \((s, i) = (762, 5)\), the removal rate is \(0.5\), the initial log contact rate is \(-6\), the contact-rate precision is \(100\) and the reporting probability is \(0.9\), with a binomial observation. The latent path is simulated with time step \(0.001\) on the interval from \(0\) to \(10\), and the filter uses substeps of length \(0.1\). Observations are taken at spacings \(1\), \(2\) and \(5\). Spacing \(1\) is the published grid. Spacings \(2\) and \(5\) are coarser, and both were fixed before any weight was computed. All algorithms were coded in R. The reported summaries are effective sample sizes of the proposal weights. The design does not target a posterior distribution of the static parameters, so there is no gold-standard posterior.

We drew \(20\) replicates, with \(500\) proposals in each window. Replicate \(r\) was initialised with seed \(20251015 + r\), for \(r = 1, \ldots, 20\). Binomial observations at spacings \(1\), \(2\) and \(5\) were drawn in that order, before the proposals. Every simulated path was retained.

For each replicate, each spacing and each guide, the within-replicate summary is the median of the window values of Equation~\eqref{eq:ess}. The reported summary is the median of those \(20\) numbers. For the linear guide the comparison is between spacing \(5\) and spacing \(1\). The myopic guide uses the same simulated paths and the same observations, with independent Poisson draws. Table~\ref{tab:s1} reports the linear guide. Figure~\ref{fig:ess} plots the linear medians, the myopic medians and the residual medians from Table~\ref{tab:s2}.

From Table~\ref{tab:s1}, the linear median effective sample size is \(149.66\) at spacing \(1\) and \(2.06\) at spacing \(5\). The coarse spacing gives the smaller value, which is the comparison specified above. At spacing \(2\) the linear median is \(8.09\). At every linear row, the median that includes zero weights equals the median computed on the finite weights alone, so the fall is present in the finite weights. The median proportion of invalid draws is \(0.033\) at spacing \(1\), \(0.009\) at spacing \(2\) and \(0\) at spacing \(5\).

The myopic median is \(139.53\) at spacing \(1\), \(142.82\) at spacing \(2\) and \(195.18\) at spacing \(5\). The myopic median increases with the spacing.

Two qualifications belong with the comparison. The guide received the simulated prevalence and the simulated contact-rate path, so the result describes \(h^{*}\) given those paths. At spacing \(1\) the linear median is \(149.66\) out of \(500\) draws, and the guide is already uneven on the published grid. The comparison is across spacings. It measures no pathwise distance to the true conditioned process.

The residual guide of Section~\ref{sec:residual} used the same mechanism, the same seeds and the same observation draws. The linear proposals from the first comparison were kept. The ordinary differential equations were integrated with a Runge--Kutta step of length \(0.1\). The comparison, specified before these weights were computed, asks whether the residual median effective sample size at spacing \(5\) exceeds the linear median \(2.06\). With two windows, the within-replicate median is the average of the two window values, the same functional as the linear summary at spacing \(5\).

From Table~\ref{tab:s2}, the residual median is \(281.71\) at spacing \(1\), \(236.47\) at spacing \(2\) and \(169.45\) at spacing \(5\). The spacing-\(5\) value exceeds \(2.06\). The residual median declines as the spacing grows, and it remains above the linear value. At spacing \(5\) the median on the finite weights is \(173.33\). The median proportion of invalid draws is \(0.029\) at spacing \(1\), \(0.010\) at spacing \(2\) and \(0.0005\) at spacing \(5\).

That spacing-\(5\) proportion is a median across replicates, and the two windows behave differently. Five of the \(20\) first windows have all \(500\) residual draws invalid. The median effective sample size of the \(20\) first windows is \(5.87\), and the median of the \(20\) second windows is \(331.99\). The comparison with \(2.06\) uses the average of the two windows. On the same spacing the linear guide has median effective sample size \(1.36\) on the first window and \(1.62\) on the second, and every linear window has a positive effective sample size. The residual median at spacing \(5\) therefore combines a weak first window, covering the rise of the outbreak, with a much stronger second window.
