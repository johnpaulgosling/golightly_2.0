\title{A diagnostic for the linear incidence guide under sparse observation}
\author{}
\date{15 October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Stochastic epidemic models of cumulative incidence are used when new infections are recorded in discrete windows and the latent transmission process is not observed. Guiding that latent path inside a particle filter is difficult, because a forward draw of incidence often misses the next count and the weights then degenerate. We give a diagnostic for the linear incidence guide that proposes those latent counts. The guide freezes the hazard over the time remaining until the next observation and then shifts it by a Gaussian approximation of the count. The importance weight keeps the discretised epidemic transition in the numerator. A poor guide changes the weights, whilst the printed target of the ratio stays that transition times the observation probability. We compare the effective sample size of this guide with a myopic proposal on repeated synthetic series, at the published observation spacing and at two coarser spacings fixed before the run. The median effective sample size of the linear guide is smaller at the coarsest spacing than at the published spacing. The myopic comparator does not fall in the same way. We do not replace the guide, and we do not analyse a surveillance series.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} conditioned hazard, effective sample size, Markov jump process, particle filter, SIR model
