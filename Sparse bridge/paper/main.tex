\title{A residual replacement for the linear incidence guide}
\author{}
\date{15 October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Stochastic epidemic models of cumulative incidence are used when new infections are recorded in discrete windows and the latent path is not observed. A linear guide proposes the latent counts by freezing the hazard until the next observation and shifting it with a Gaussian approximation of the count. The importance weight keeps the discretised transition in the numerator. On repeated synthetic series the median effective sample size of this guide falls from 149.66 at the published spacing to 2.06 when the spacing is five times coarser. A myopic proposal does not fall in the same way. We replace the Poisson means by the increment of a restarted linear-noise path, together with a conditional residual mean for infections. The denominator remains a product of Poisson probabilities for the same increments. At the coarse spacing the residual median effective sample size is 169.45. The residual median still declines as the spacing grows, and at the coarse spacing the gain is concentrated in the second window. In five of 20 replicates every draw in the first window has weight zero. The study uses a synthetic susceptible-infected-removed mechanism.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} conditioned hazard, effective sample size, Markov jump process, particle filter, SIR model
