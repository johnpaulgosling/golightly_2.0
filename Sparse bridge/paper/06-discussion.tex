\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We have given a reading of the linear incidence guide in \citet{whitaker2025} and a comparison of its weights under coarser observation. The constant-hazard step is the linearity assumption named in their limitations section. On the printed importance weight, a poor conditioned hazard changes the proposal and the weights. On the synthetic mechanism of their Section 4.1, the median effective sample size of the linear guide is \(2.06\) at spacing \(5\) and \(149.66\) at spacing \(1\) (Table~\ref{tab:s1}). The myopic comparator increases over the same spacings. The guide was given the simulated state and the simulated contact-rate path, and the published spacing already yields a median well below the number of draws.

The comparison is useful when an observation window is lengthened and the question is whether this proposal is the source of uneven weights. A posterior for the static parameters is outside its scope. The three spacings were chosen before the comparison because they divide the published horizon, and they are a design choice rather than estimated change-points.

The residual guide in Section~\ref{sec:residual} replaces the Poisson means inside that proposal. The new means are the increment of a restarted incidence linear-noise path, plus a conditional residual mean on the infection count. The denominator of Equation~\eqref{eq:weight} remains the product of Poisson probabilities for those increments. The median effective sample size at spacing \(5\) is \(169.45\), set against the linear median \(2.06\). The same summary declines from \(281.71\) at spacing \(1\). The gain at spacing \(5\) is uneven across the two windows. The first window, which covers the rise of the outbreak, has median effective sample size \(5.87\), and in five replicates every draw in that window has weight zero. The second window has median \(331.99\). A guide that improves the second half of a sparsely observed outbreak, and still fails on the first half, is only a partial replacement.

Equation (19) of \citet{golightly2019} samples a Markov jump process under a Gaussian observation of the state, with weight (3). Theorem 4.2 of \citet{corstanje2023} conditions an inhomogeneous Poisson process on an exact integer end-point, and its ratio (16) contains \(h(0, x_0)\). The density \(q\) in this paper is the Poisson product of Section~\ref{sec:residual}.

The comparison uses the synthetic mechanism of Section 4.1 of \citet{whitaker2025}. The weekly Ebola series and the weekly COVID-19 series discussed in that paper would need the counts in reproducible form before they could be analysed in the same way.
