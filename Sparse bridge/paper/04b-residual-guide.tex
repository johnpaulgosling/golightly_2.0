\section{Residual incidence guide}
\label{sec:residual}

The residual guide changes the Poisson means and keeps the Poisson draw, so that \(q\) is the product of Poisson probabilities on the same incidence increments that appear in the numerator of Equation~\eqref{eq:weight}. The numerator itself is unchanged.

The limitations paragraph of \citet{whitaker2025} points to constructions that sample different objects. Equation (19) of \citet{golightly2019} samples a Markov jump process, and the weight (3) is the jump-process likelihood ratio under a Gaussian observation of the state. Theorem 4.2 of \citet{corstanje2023} conditions an inhomogeneous Poisson process on an exact integer end-point, and the ratio (16) contains the transition probability \(h(0, x_0)\). Corollary 4.3.6 and Theorem 4.4.2 of \citet{corstanje2024} are path-space ratios for a guided chemical reaction process conditioned on an exact end constraint. Equations (15) and (25) of \citet{whitaker2017} are Gaussian densities of a diffusion skeleton. The denominator used here is the Poisson product described above, and the factor \(h(0, x_0)\) remains inside ratio (16) wherever that ratio is discussed.

At a substep, the linear-noise approximation of cumulative incidence is restarted at the current prevalence. The incidence coordinate \(\eta\) starts at zero, the fundamental matrix \(G\) starts at the identity, and the residual variance \(V\) starts at zero. The contact rate is held at its current value. We integrate equations (4), (8) and (9) of \citet{golightly2023} over the next substep and over the time remaining until the observation. The Jacobian is the matrix printed after their equation (6). The restart is the one they describe after their equation (19). \citet[Section 4.2]{fearnhead2014} state that restart: the ordinary differential equation is re-initialised at the beginning of each interval. The integration uses a classical Runge--Kutta method with step length 0.1, chosen before the comparison in Section~\ref{sec:simulation}.

The mean of the binomial observation then uses the linear-noise mean of the remaining infections in place of the frozen product \(h_{\mathrm{inf}}(t_i - \tau)\). The variance remains the binomial variance in Equation~\eqref{eq:hstar}, \(\sigma^{2} = (1 - \rho)\hat{\mu}\). For an observation of infections this is the variance in equation (22) of \citet{golightly2023}.

Let \(R\) denote the residual incidence about \(\eta\), with variance \(V\) after the restart. The covariance between the residual at the next substep and the residual at the end of the remaining interval is \(V_{\Delta\tau}(G_{t_i - \tau} G_{\Delta\tau}^{-1})'\). \citet[Section 3.1.2]{whitaker2017} write the same block as \(P_t\psi_t P_T'\) after their display (24). The observation loads on the infection component by \(\rho\). The conditional mean of the infection residual at the next substep is that infection covariance, times \(\rho(y - \hat{\mu})\), divided by \(\rho^{2} V_{11}(t_i - \tau) + \sigma^{2}\). The removal mean is the ordinary-differential increment of the removal coordinate, in line with Equation~\eqref{eq:hstar}, which corrects the infection component only.

The Poisson mean for the next infection increment is the ordinary-differential increment of \(\eta\) plus that conditional residual mean. The Poisson mean for the removal increment is the ordinary-differential increment of the removal coordinate alone. A draw receives weight zero if the integration stops because a prevalence on the ordinary differential equation would be negative, if the absolute value of the determinant of \(G_{\Delta\tau}\) is smaller than \(10^{-12}\), if either Poisson mean is negative or is not finite, or if the updated counts would be negative. The truncation in Section 3.2 of \citet{golightly2019} applies to their equation (5), and a negative Poisson mean here is left unchanged.

Equation (6) of \citet{golightly2018} is the constant-hazard correction that \citet{whitaker2025} adapt. The means in this section are a separate construction. \citet{fintzi2022} reparameterise a linear-noise approximation for incidence counts. The equations integrated here are those printed by \citet{golightly2023}, who cite that reparameterisation.
