\label{sec:related}

Equation (9) of \citet{golightly2015} is the constant-hazard step that \citet{whitaker2025} adapt. The observation in the 2015 paper is a Gaussian function of the state, and the observation in the incidence filter is a binomial or negative binomial count. Equation (5) of \citet{golightly2019} restates that hazard. Their Section 3.2 says that normality of the remaining reaction counts, and a hazard that is constant on the whole remaining interval, are often unreasonable. Their Section 6 says that treating the time until the next observation as one discretisation makes the bridges less accurate as the inter-observation time increases. Equation (19) of the same paper is a separate proposal: a jump-process bridge under a Gaussian observation of the state, with weight (3). Its density is the jump-process likelihood ratio, and the incidence filter requires a product of Poisson probabilities on a grid.

Theorem 4.2 of \citet{corstanje2023} conditions an inhomogeneous Poisson process on an exact integer end-point. The likelihood ratio (16) contains \(h(0,x_0)\), the transition probability of the original process. Their Section 4.1 does not treat a binomial observation of an increment on a grid. The proposal in Section~\ref{sec:residual} uses the incidence linear-noise equations of \citet{golightly2023} and the residual covariance block of \citet[Section 3.1.2]{whitaker2017}, and it evaluates a Poisson probability for the same increments as the numerator.

\citet{lowe2023} accelerate particle Markov chain Monte Carlo for a chemical kinetic model. Their conditioned hazard multiplies a jump hazard by a ratio of observation densities, and they replace that density by a linear-noise Gaussian. The construction follows \citet{golightly2019}, and it differs from the incidence proposal in \citet{whitaker2025}. They remark that nonlinear behaviour between observations precludes a modified diffusion bridge that pushes particles towards the observations in a linear fashion. For that bridge they name Durham and Gallant (2002), and for a residual bridge they name \citet{whitaker2017}. The residual covariance in Section~\ref{sec:residual} is the block in Section 3.1.2 of the latter paper.

\citet{jovanovski2024} build data-conditional proposals for approximate Bayesian computation on a diffusion. Their discussion of erratic paths is a conjecture about where their speed-up is largest, and it concerns their own proposals. \citet[pp.~2--3]{whitaker2025} also cite \citet{golightly2018}. Equation (6) of that paper is the constant-hazard correction adapted by the 2025 guide. Section~\ref{sec:residual} uses a different mean.
